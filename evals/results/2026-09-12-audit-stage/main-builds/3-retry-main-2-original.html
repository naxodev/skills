Cedar Relay retries only eligible failures

A retryability check stops permanent failures, while exponential delays spread out further attempts. Delivery can still produce duplicate effects.

Source walkthrough · Deterministic modeled probes

1 · The settingOne send, then a limited number of retries

Cedar Relay wraps an asynchronous delivery operation, send, with a bounded retry loop. You supply retries as a nonnegative integer and sleep as an asynchronous wait. A successful send returns immediately; a rejection enters the retry path.

The budget counts extra attempts. With two retries, the loop can call send three times. Previously, every rejection received the same treatment: request a 10-millisecond wait, then try again unless the budget was exhausted.

2 · The gapPermanent failures kept going

Send errors already carry a boolean retryable property, but the old loop ignored it. In the supplied permanent-failure probe, the first send rejects with retryable: false. The old implementation waits and makes a second call, which the model allows to succeed. The new implementation stops after one call and propagates the permanent error.

This removes an attempt that the error contract says should not happen. It does not establish the author's claimed exactly-once delivery guarantee.

3 · The mechanismCheck eligibility before waiting

The catch branch now uses two reasons to stop:

A non-retryable error exits immediately. A retryable error also exits on the final allowed attempt, with no further wait. Otherwise, the loop requests an increasing delay:

Because attempt starts at zero, requested waits are 10, 20, then 40 milliseconds as the budget permits. The exhaustion probe records three sends and waits of 10 and 20, replacing 10 and 10. These are requested delays: the probe's sleep resolves immediately and measures no elapsed time.

A zero retry budget still permits one send. If sleep rejects, its error propagates and prevents another send.

4 · The short versionBetter retry decisions, no duplicate protection

The change filters failures and increases waits within the existing budget. A rejection can still follow a committed remote effect: the duplicate probe records two effects after a retry. There is no deduplication or idempotency contract. Separate calls also share neither a counter nor a lock; the concurrent probe records an effect from each call. Retry policy improves, but exactly-once delivery remains unsupported.
