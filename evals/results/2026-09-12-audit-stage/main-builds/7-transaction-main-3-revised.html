Lantern Ledger · Change walkthrough

Commit the payment before sending the notification

Moving publication past the commit prevents notifications for rolled-back payments, but leaves delivery as a separate operation.

Fictional component study · Source and in-memory probe results

1 · The settingTwo operations, one transaction boundary

Lantern Ledger marks a payment as paid and publishes a notification. Previously, both calls ran inside the database transaction callback. The database commits staged writes only after that callback fulfills. Publication, however, is external: placing it inside the callback does not make the notification part of the database transaction.

2 · The gapA notification could outlive a rollback

If publication succeeded and the commit then failed, the payment rolled back while its notification remained visible. The supplied probe records exactly that outcome: an unpaid record and one notification. A publication error after external delivery could produce the same mismatch, because rejecting the callback rolled back only the database writes.

3 · The changed mechanismWait for the commit, then publish

The changed function first awaits the transaction:

That await completes after commit, not merely after markPaid. A write or commit rejection exits the function before publication. In the supplied in-memory results, a failed commit now leaves zero notifications.

Publication follows outside the callback:

A publication rejection still rejects the function, but cannot undo the committed payment. The probes show both possibilities: rejection before delivery leaves zero notifications; rejection after delivery leaves one. The author describes payment and notification as atomic, but this code establishes ordering, not an all-or-nothing result.

4 · The short versionA stronger order, not guaranteed delivery

Failed payment transactions no longer trigger publication. A process stop between commit and publication can still leave a paid record without a notification; no durable outbox or background retry is supplied. Retrying after uncertain delivery can duplicate notifications. Idempotent markPaid protects the payment write, not delivery: both versions produce two notifications under concurrent calls in the probe. These results support the ordering change, not exactly-once delivery or atomicity.

Based on the supplied source and probes. No real database or process-kill simulation was performed.
